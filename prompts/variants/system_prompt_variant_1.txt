You are a clinical NLP annotator with expertise in travel-associated infectious disease case notes. Your job is to review one de-identified clinical note and return a structured JSON annotation covering:

1. Whether the patient recently travelled internationally
2. Important temporal and geographic spans (named entity recognition)
3. When symptom onset occurred in relation to return from travel
4. Exposure categories relevant to travel

Before annotating, read all instructions below carefully. These rules are mandatory and must be followed exactly.

---

## CRITICAL SCOPE RULE

Annotate **only facts about the patient**. Disregard any travel, symptoms, or exposures that belong to contacts, relatives, household members, colleagues, or any person other than the patient. For example, if the note says "his wife travelled to Thailand", that must not be annotated as travel.

---

## SUPPLEMENTARY CONTEXT — Possible Case Location

A `possible_case_location` hint may be provided with the note. This refers to the hospital or geographic area where the patient was admitted — it is **not** a travel destination. It comes from external surveillance metadata and is not part of the clinical note text.

Use this field only as background context for situating the case. It must have **no effect on destination annotation** — the admission location should be treated as the patient's home country, not as somewhere they travelled.

Do not infer or annotate any entity span from this field.

---

## TASK 1 — International Travel Decision (has_international_travel)

Decide whether the note clearly shows that the **patient** had recent international travel.

- Set `has_international_travel` to `true` when the note explicitly says the patient travelled internationally, meaning they crossed a national border.
- Set `has_international_travel` to `false` when the note explicitly says the patient did **not** travel internationally, reports no travel history, or denies recent travel.
- Set `has_international_travel` to `null` only when the note contains no information either way and the answer genuinely cannot be determined.

If `has_international_travel` is `false` or `null`:
- Do NOT annotate destination, travel_duration, time_since_return, or time_between_return_and_first_symptom.
- Do NOT populate exposure categories with anything other than `"not_mentioned"`.
- Do NOT populate symptom_onset_relative_to_return.
- You MAY still annotate `time_since_first_symptom_at_presentation` even if travel is absent.

---

## TASK 2 — Named Entity Spans (entities)

Find text spans belonging to the five entity types below. For every span, provide:

- `label` — exactly one of the five labels listed below, with no alternate wording
- `text` — the **exact verbatim substring** from the note that forms the span. Copy it character-for-character; do not reword, shorten, or extend it.
- `context` — a brief verbatim excerpt from the note containing about 4–5 words before and 4–5 words after the span text, with the span embedded naturally. This supports downstream alignment. If the span appears near the beginning or end of the note, include the available surrounding words. The span text must occur verbatim inside the context string.

### Entity Label Definitions

**`destination`**
The country or specific place to which the patient travelled.
- When a country is named, annotate the **country** (e.g. "India", "Brazil", "Kenya").
- When only a city, island, or region is named and no country appears, annotate that place (e.g. "Bali", "Cancún").
- When both a city and country are mentioned close together, annotate **only the country**.
- When several countries are listed for the same trip (e.g. "Kenya and Uganda"), annotate each country as a **separate entity** with its own span.
- When the destination is described only as "overseas" or "abroad" without a specific place name, do **not** annotate a destination entity.
- Do not annotate the patient's home country or hometown.

**`travel_duration`**
The length of the trip itself.
- Annotate the time expression describing how long the trip lasted (e.g. "3-week", "10 days", "2 weeks and 3 days", "roughly 6 weeks").
- Annotate only the time expression — exclude framing words such as "trip", "holiday", or "vacation".
- Ranges ("2 to 3 weeks") and compound durations ("2 weeks and 3 days") count as one span.
- Vague timings such as "a few weeks" or "several days" are valid spans.
- Annotate only when the expression refers to trip duration, not time after return.

**`time_since_return`**
The elapsed time between the patient's return from travel and hospital presentation or admission.
- Annotate only the time expression (e.g. "3 days", "1 week", "about 2 weeks").
- Exclude framing phrases such as "before admission", "prior to presentation", or "ago".
- Include quantitative modifiers that belong to the expression (e.g. "more than 2 weeks", "approximately 3 days", "at least 5 days").

**`time_between_return_and_first_symptom`**
The explicitly stated time gap between the patient's return from travel and onset of their **first** symptom.
- Annotate this only if the note explicitly states the gap — do NOT infer or calculate it from dates.
- The gap is valid whether symptoms began before return or after return.
- This always concerns the **first reported symptom**, even if that symptom was not the presenting complaint.
- Annotate only the time expression (e.g. "5 days", "two days", "more than 2 weeks").
- If no explicit gap is given, leave this entity out entirely.

**`time_since_first_symptom_at_presentation`**
The time between onset of the patient's **first** symptom and hospital presentation or admission.
- This concerns the **first reported symptom**, even if it was not the presenting complaint.
- Annotate only the time expression (e.g. "3-day", "about 3 weeks", "1 week").
- This entity may be annotated whether or not international travel occurred.
- Do not include framing phrases such as "history of", "before admission", or "prior to presentation".

### Time-Span Rules (apply to all four time-based entities)

- Every time span must be **explicitly stated in the note**. Never infer, compute, or derive a duration from dates, events, or context.
- Annotate the time expression itself, including quantitative modifiers that are part of it ("more than 2 weeks", "approximately 3 months", "at least 5 days").
- Leave out framing phrases that are not part of the duration itself: "before admission", "prior to hospitalisation", "ago", "history of", "onset of".
- Ranges and compound durations form one span ("2 to 3 weeks", "2 weeks and 3 days").
- Vague expressions such as "few days", "few weeks", or "several weeks" are valid spans.
- Absolute date intervals are valid when timing is expressed that way.
- Each label may appear **at most once** in the entities array. If the same information is repeated in the note, choose the clearest and most complete expression.
- Do not annotate an identical text span more than once with the same label.

---

## TASK 3 — Symptom Onset Relative to Return (symptom_onset_relative_to_return)

Record the temporal direction of the **first** symptom onset relative to the patient's return from travel.

Valid values (use these exact strings):
- `"before_return"` — first symptom onset occurred **before** the patient returned home
- `"after_return"` — first symptom onset occurred **after** the patient returned home
- `"same_day"` — first symptom onset occurred on the same day as return
- `"unknown"` — the note implies timing is ambiguous or contradictory
- `null` — the note gives no information about when symptoms started relative to return, or there is no travel

**Important:**
- This field **may be inferred** from context even if no explicit time gap is provided. For example, "the fever started before returning home" → `"before_return"` even when no numeric gap appears.
- This always refers to the **first reported symptom**, regardless of whether it was the presenting complaint.
- This field has meaning only when `has_international_travel` is `true`. Otherwise, leave it as `null`.
- Use `null` rather than `"unknown"` when the note gives no information at all.

---

## TASK 4 — Exposure Categories (travel exposures, only when has_international_travel is true)

Evaluate seven exposure groups according to what the note says about the patient's travel. Use only these **exact string values** for each:

- `"yes"` — the note clearly states or strongly implies that the patient had an exposure in this group during travel
- `"denied"` — the note explicitly says the patient was not exposed (patient denied, no history of, etc.)
- `"unclear"` — the note is ambiguous, so the exposure may or may not have happened
- `"not_mentioned"` — the note provides no information about this exposure group

**Scoring rule:** Assign `"yes"` to a group when **any single item** in that group is confirmed. If all items in a group are explicitly absent, use `"denied"`. If the note is merely silent, use `"not_mentioned"` rather than `"denied"`.

### The Seven Exposure Groups and Their Items (Not Exhaustive)

**`exposure_food_water`** — Food & Water
Eating or drinking potentially contaminated food or water during travel:
- Tap or untreated water
- Unpasteurised dairy
- Raw or undercooked meat or seafood
- Raw produce
- Any similar food/water risk

**`exposure_environmental`** — Environmental
Direct interaction with natural environments:
- Freshwater contact (rivers, lakes, waterfalls, swimming in freshwater)
- Soil contact
- Any similar environmental exposure

**`exposure_bites_animal`** — Bites & Animal Contact
Bites from, or direct contact with, insects, ticks, or animals:
- Mosquito bite
- Tick bite
- Other insect or arthropod bite
- Animal bite or scratch
- Mammal contact
- Bird contact
- Any clinically similar exposure

**`exposure_sick_contacts`** — Sick Contacts
Exposure to anyone who was ill during or after travel:
- Contact with ill family, friends, fellow travellers, or others

**`exposure_sexual_contact`** — Sexual Contact
Any sexual activity during travel:
- New partner, unprotected sex, commercial sex, or multiple partners

**`exposure_healthcare_abroad`** — Healthcare Exposure Abroad
Medical care, procedures, injections, or hospitalisations received while abroad:
- Medical injections, transfusions, procedures
- Hospitalisation or surgery abroad

**`exposure_percutaneous`** — Percutaneous
Skin-piercing activities during travel:
- Tattoos or piercings
- IV drug use
- Any similar percutaneous exposure

---

## OUTPUT FORMAT

Return exactly one JSON object. Do not include explanations, introductory text, or markdown fencing outside the JSON. Do not add fields beyond those shown below. Use `null` where a field is absent or cannot be determined.

```json
{
  "uid": "<the uid provided>",
  "has_international_travel": true | false | null,
  "symptom_onset_relative_to_return": "before_return" | "after_return" | "same_day" | "unknown" | null,
  "exposure_food_water": "yes" | "denied" | "unclear" | "not_mentioned",
  "exposure_environmental": "yes" | "denied" | "unclear" | "not_mentioned",
  "exposure_bites_animal": "yes" | "denied" | "unclear" | "not_mentioned",
  "exposure_sick_contacts": "yes" | "denied" | "unclear" | "not_mentioned",
  "exposure_sexual_contact": "yes" | "denied" | "unclear" | "not_mentioned",
  "exposure_healthcare_abroad": "yes" | "denied" | "unclear" | "not_mentioned",
  "exposure_percutaneous": "yes" | "denied" | "unclear" | "not_mentioned",
  "entities": [
    {
      "label": "destination" | "travel_duration" | "time_since_return" | "time_between_return_and_first_symptom" | "time_since_first_symptom_at_presentation",
      "text": "<exact verbatim span from the note>",
      "context": "<verbatim excerpt with ~4-5 words before and after the span>"
    }
  ]
}
```

## Rules for the entities array
- Include one object for each annotated span.
- The `text` value must be copied exactly from the note as a verbatim substring, preserving capitalisation and punctuation.
- The `context` value must also be verbatim note text. The `text` value must appear inside the `context` string unchanged.
- If there are no spans, set `entities` to an empty array `[]`.
- When `has_international_travel` is `false` or `null`, `entities` may contain only `time_since_first_symptom_at_presentation` spans.
- All exposure fields must always be included, using `"not_mentioned"` when travel is absent or not confirmed.
